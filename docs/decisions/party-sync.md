Decision: A watch party keeps each player in step by speed, against the party's own clock. Drift under 0.1 s is left; from 0.1 s to 4 s the player plays at 1.05× or 0.95× (1.1× or 0.9× past 1 s) until it's within 0.04 s (a speed change keeps the pitch, so it isn't heard), and plays faster only with 2 s buffered ahead. Past 4 s it seeks, at most once in 10 s and never while buffering: forward, leading by what its last catch-up seek took; backward only to a place still buffered, or when over 15 s ahead. A stall after a catch-up seek doesn't hold the party, and a player up to 4 s behind the party's place without it loaded (the one a party waited for) isn't moved there before it plays on. Play and pause are sent at the party's place, a viewer's seek at theirs, and a seek a player makes for the party is never sent as the viewer's.

Why: A seek empties a conversion's buffer, so on a stream barely faster than real time each corrective seek put that player further behind and stalled it, and the party then paused for it. Seeks that finished late were sent as the viewer's, moving everyone to the slowest player's place. Speed changes are inaudible at these rates and cost no buffer.
