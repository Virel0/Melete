Decision: `melete import aoide` finds a record's track by its content key (`artist|album|title|seconds`) as before first. When that finds nothing, it tries each artist the key names (split on commas, `;`, `/`, " & ", " feat. " and the like), and each artist a track is credited to (the parts of its artist and album-artist tags, and the artists Melete credits it to) stands for it. When the key's album still fits nothing, the artist's tracks of that title on other albums within a second of its length are looked at: one is taken, and several are narrowed to the one whose album name holds the key's or is held in it, then to the one of exactly its length. Those found this way count as near matches in the report; anything still tied is listed as ambiguous and its record left as it was. Playlist artwork that was a Jellyfin item is still cleared, not fetched.

Why: The apps built the key from Jellyfin's view of the tags, which lists a track's artists separately and names an album by its edition, so a track credited to several artists, or on a renamed edition, went unmatched though only one track fits. Length and album name are what the key carries to tell two candidates apart; the sidecar's records carry no file path, so a tie they don't break stays a tie. Fetching the old artwork would mean connecting to the old server with its key, a new outside connection that needs its own decision.
