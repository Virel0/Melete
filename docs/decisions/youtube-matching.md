Decision: Amends decision 340. When a song's YouTube results are judged, an artist's name counts as named in a video's title or as its channel with or without its leading article ("Harbour Choir" for "The Harbour Choir"), and names and titles are compared with Cyrillic letters read as Latin ones (а a, ж zh, х kh, ч ch, ш sh, щ shch, ю yu, я ya and so on), both sides alike. When none fits, the song's word names the result that missed on the fewest counts, its channel, and what it missed (its title, the artist, another version, or its length against the song's). When the chosen video's audio won't download, the next result that fits is fetched at once; only when none is left is it a failure tried again a day later.

Why: Requests often credit "The …" where uploads don't, and Russian and Ukrainian music is often requested in one script and uploaded in the other, so right videos were turned down. Saying which result came closest and why lets an admin see whether matching was too strict or the song isn't there. A refused download (YouTube's 403s, which come and go with its players) is about that video, so another upload of the song is worth trying before waiting a day.
