Decision: A motion cover made from a still cover keeps the camera still: it breathes slowly in
and back once over a 16-second loop (the edge moving in by 1.5% of the cover's side at the
deepest, nearer things more than farther ones). Where the cover has a distant part (the farthest
fifth of its depth) that is soft (few sharp edges), away from lettering and faces, mostly one
piece, and between 12% and 70% of the cover, that part alone sways a little (0.4% of the
side, twice a loop, each place at its own moment). Elsewhere nothing drifts. A cover's colours
also carry its palette: its main colours, each at least 3% of it and clearly apart. Melete makes
no tall video: an app draws a full-screen player from the square cover or motion cover and the
palette.

Why: Motion covers made by artists keep the camera still and move only parts of the picture, and
the circle moved the whole cover, which drew the eye. A still camera with a slow breath reads as
depth without that. Only soft distant parts drift because sharp ones (shelves, buildings, lights)
wobble visibly, and scattered patches would shimmer; checked on nine real covers of many kinds.
Full-screen players either show the art full width fading into its colours or the cover over a
blurred copy of itself, both drawn by the app; a video with the layout made in would rarely
match the app's.
