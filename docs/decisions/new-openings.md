Decision: Openings and endings are told apart, per series and across its seasons, and automatic skipping leaves alone one a user hasn't heard. When an intro or credits is found by matching episodes, the start of its fingerprint (60 seconds at most, so 7.5 KB a song) is compared with those of the series' openings, or endings, already known, with the same comparison that finds them; one sharing at least half of the shorter (10 seconds at least) is the same song and gives its id, otherwise the segment gets a new id kept with its fingerprint. Each user's seen songs are kept: the player says so once a new one has played through to its end (not after a seek or a Skip), and anyone who had already played a file past the part, or finished it, when it was told apart counts as having seen it. Segments carry `new`, true for a song the asking user hasn't seen; automatic skipping passes over only what isn't new, and Skip still works. Without automatic skipping nothing changes. Segments found before this have no id and are never new, so nothing changes for them until they're found again. A chapter's intro or credits takes the newness of what listening found in the same file; credits found by the file's ending alone are never new.

Why: In anime above all, a new season's opening or one that changes partway through a season is part of the show, and skipping it unheard loses it. Telling songs apart by sound alone needs no outside service and reuses what detection already decodes; keeping only the start of each song bounds what's stored. Counting what a user had already played past keeps the change from replaying openings they've heard. A segment the player can't recognise is treated as heard, so a missing id never makes automatic skipping stop working.
