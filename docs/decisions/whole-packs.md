Decision: Amends decision 327. A pack of several seasons grabbed by a season's search is taken for that season as before (its part, with any upgrades), and for each other monitored season it holds that it adds missing episodes to, as that season's own pack would be taken: those episodes only, never an upgrade, so no file on disk is replaced before its copy is in place (`downloads.seasons`, {"<season>": [episodes]}). Those seasons count as on their way for searches, syncs, Search all missing, progress and stalls while it downloads. It's brought home one season at a time: the season it was grabbed for first, then the lowest other one left, each pass that season's files only, into its own folder and checked against its own episodes' running times; a pass that finds none of its season's files goes on to the next, and a running-time failure fails and blocklists the whole pack as before. When nothing of the searched season is here and what the pack adds can't be told (no episode list), it's taken whole for that season, as before. Packs imported before for one season, whose files are still on the seedbox (not removed from Deluge with their data), are opened again once, at start and hourly, for the monitored seasons they hold missing episodes of. And of accepted releases of the same quality rank, one holding fewer seasons is grabbed first.

Why: Bringing home only the season a pack was grabbed for left the rest of a large download unused on the seedbox, and the other seasons' searches then grabbed further packs, so a series could be downloaded several times over for one season each. One season at a time keeps every file in its own folder and checked against its own runtimes, as decision 327 wanted, with the code that already does that for one season. Taking only missing episodes for the other seasons keeps decision 37's rule that a file is replaced only by a confirmed upgrade. Preferring a season's own pack avoids a far larger download when one as good as it exists.
