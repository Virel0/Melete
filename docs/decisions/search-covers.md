Decision: Albums and songs not in the library get a cover in searches for music to request only with `MELETE_SEARCH_COVERS` on (off by default, and only while MusicBrainz is on): their release group's front cover from the Cover Art Archive, a song's from its single's release group, else its album's (as MusicBrainz's search lists the releases it's on; a plain one before a compilation or live one). Kept to discovery posters' terms: a search notes the release groups it lists, the cover route serves only those, for 30 days after they were last listed, fetching each once (500 pixels, at most 10 MiB) and caching it under the release group's id with the sizes made from it; housekeeping forgets older ones and all but the newest 2000 with their files, and every one when the setting is off. When the archive has none, or fails to give it, that's believed for a day: the route doesn't ask again and searches don't offer it. An album or song in a library hidden from a user is, to them, like any not in the library: it's offered, listed and served the same way, so nothing tells them it's there. What leaves the server: each such release group's id, to coverartarchive.org and the archive.org hosts it redirects to, over https, with Melete's usual User-Agent.

Why: Music results without covers are hard to tell apart, and the apps show one when there is one. A release group's id says nothing about the household; tying each cover to a list Melete answered, a time limit and a cap keeps the route from becoming an open proxy for the archive, as decision 111 does for TMDB's posters. Answering a hidden album differently from an absent one would tell a limited user what a hidden library holds. Remembering a failure keeps every view from asking again.
