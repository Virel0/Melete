Decision: Admins see every peer of Melete's own tunnel now (`GET /api/v1/tunnel/peers`) and remove any of them (`DELETE /api/v1/tunnel/peers/{address}`), and nobody else does: both are admin routes, which a household device reaches only with the admin part and an admin's sign-in. Each peer comes with whose it is (a device and its user, or a guest, their party and who invited them), its last handshake, bytes in and out and whether it's connected (a handshake within 3 minutes) from WireGuard, its open connections as the tunnel's TCP/IP stack counts them for its per-peer limit (a count the stack shares after each turn, read without waiting on it), and what it's doing, from the requests the tunnel's gate lets through: watching (a video stream or transcode), listening (a track's stream), in a party (a guest's routes), downloading (an offline copy) for 90 seconds after such a request, else browsing. The outside address a peer connects from is read from WireGuard when asked and given only while it's connected; it isn't written to the database, the logs or any answer but this admin one, and no key is in the answer. Removing a device's peer deletes its row too, so it doesn't come back at the next start; removing a guest's takes the guest out of their party as sending them away does, or, before anyone joined through it, withdraws its invite.

Why: An admin should be able to see who is reaching Melete from outside and cut anyone off at once, without the household's whereabouts being kept anywhere. A connection's outside address says where someone is, so it's shown only while it's live and only to admins. Without the sticky window a player's progress reports would make a peer watching a film look like it's browsing.
