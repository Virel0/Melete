Decision: Amends decision 297: a search gets five Soulseek downloads at most for its album or song, the first included, not three. And Soulseek is searched again for what it couldn't give: a wanted song it was searched for that has no download but failed ones, and a monitored album (of a monitored artist) with a failed Soulseek download and none on its way, a day after the last search or download of it (for a song, of the album fetched for it too) and a day after the last search again, three times at most, and only while nothing is on its way or in the library (a song from YouTube, say). Each is searched as its own search would be (an album asks its indexers too), and the best accepted result is grabbed, passing over the Soulseek users a download of it already failed with; a download that fails gets its next sources as any does. Runs hourly, the first ten minutes after starting, ten searches at most a run, one at a time, the newest wanted first, songs before albums, only with slskd set up. An album never had from anyone is left to RSS syncs and Search all missing.

Why: Most failed Soulseek downloads are one user's doing (a refusal, a ban, a file changed since the search, a timeout), so a few more sources cost little and often succeed. Who shares what changes as people come and go, so a search a day later can find what the first couldn't; three times keeps a song or album that isn't there from being searched for forever, and leaving out albums never had keeps their indexers from being asked every day.
