Decision: Melete can get the certificate for its own HTTPS from an ACME CA (Let's Encrypt) and renew it, off until set up as an outside service. The domain is proved by the DNS challenge through deSEC, so the certificate is a wildcard with the bare name; the CA's staging server is used until an admin switches to the real one. The account's key is made from the OS's randomness and kept sealed, one account per CA directory, and its URI is shown for a CAA accounturi record. The certificate and its key are written to the config folder, where HTTPS reads them and takes a new pair up at once; certificate files given in the environment are refused beside it. It is renewed in the window the CA's renewal information suggests, else two-thirds through its life, with retries further and further apart kept across restarts, and a renewal that fails alerts admins (again at most every three days while it fails), saying when the certificate in use runs out.

Why: A household passing HTTPS through a VPS then needs nothing else at home to get and renew a certificate, and the certificate never sits on the VPS. With a wildcard, Certificate Transparency logs don't list the name Melete is reached at, and the DNS challenge needs nothing opened at home. Staging first keeps first tries clear of the real server's rate limits. The CA no longer sends expiry emails, so the alert is what warns before HTTPS stops working.
