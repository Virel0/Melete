Decision: An intro or credits found by matching episodes (decision 108) has its edges trimmed inward with a stricter test after the half-second one finds it: it starts and ends only where three windows of 4 hashes in a row (an eighth of a second each) average at most 7 bit errors of 32, or 2 more than the stretch's own typical window when two episodes' frames fall differently on the same audio, and never looser than the half-second test's 11. A stretch with nowhere that close is kept as found. Each scan now keeps the way it was done (`media_segment_scans.version`, 2 for trimmed edges; scans before are 1): files looked at an older way are looked at again by the same background job, one file at a time, after any file never looked at or changed, and what was found the old way is still served until then. Looked at again, a file's intro and credits get their opening or ending ids, so segments found before ids existed get them in the background; anyone who had already played past the part, or finished the file, counts as having seen it, so nothing changes for them.

Why: Where an opening's music starts under the scene's last lines, the music is shared but the lines aren't, and the loose average still counts them as shared, so automatic skipping cut dialogue; credits could run into a scene after them the same way. Frame by frame, lines over music differ clearly more than the same music does. Holding the edges to the stretch's own closeness keeps the test from cutting into the same music in two encodings whose frames don't line up. Without a version, a file is looked at once, so the fix would reach only new episodes.
