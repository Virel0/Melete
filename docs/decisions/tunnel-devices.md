Decision: Household devices get permanent peers on Melete's own WireGuard tunnel, kept in the database (`tunnel_devices`): each is one user's, named, with its public key, a preshared key Melete makes from the OS's randomness and keeps sealed (bound to the device's public key, refused plain once the database keeps secrets sealed), a fixed address in the tunnel's range that it keeps across restarts, and the parts it reaches (video, music, requests, admin; never a watch party guest's). An app makes its own key pair and sends only the public key; without one, Melete makes the pair and hands the config once, keeping only the public half. A user has at most 10 devices, held by the database, and the tunnel's 250 peers still apply; guests' and devices' peers share the range, and an address kept for a device that couldn't come back is skipped. The tunnel's gate lets a device's peer reach `Part::Always` and its own parts and nothing else, whatever the ways in say (they still apply after it), and every signed-in request through it must be its own user's, an admin's included; a guest's peer signs in no household user. Through a device's own peer, a new device may reach no more than that one does. A user removes their own devices and an admin anyone's; the peer goes at once, and the sweep that checks guests' peers every two seconds removes devices whose rows are gone, so deleting a user cuts their devices off too. A device that can't come back when the tunnel starts (its address outside `MELETE_TUNNEL_NET`, or its preshared key unreadable with this secrets key) is left off with a warning, to be removed and added again. Stream links carry their own token and aren't tied to a device's user.

Why: Devices away from home should reach Melete through the same tunnel guests use, with no router or container changes, and each only as far as its app needs, so a lost phone with a music app reaches music and nothing more. Keeping the private key on the device means a database or backup holds nothing that impersonates it. Holding each device to its own user means a config passed on can't be used with someone else's sign-in.
