Decision: Someone with no account joins a watch party as its guest. Anyone in a party may make an invite: a random secret, stored hashed, that lets one guest in once within a day and is spent as they join; the link carries it after `#`, so opening it sends it to no server or proxy. Joining gives a pass for that party alone, also random and stored hashed, kept as an HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict cookie for `/api/v1/party` only (or sent as a bearer token by an app); a change made by cookie needs the `X-Melete-Web` header, as a household sign-in's does. A pass is no sign-in: every account route refuses it, and the guest routes refuse every account token and API key (the contract tests know guest routes as their own kind and hold them to that). With it a guest follows the party, plays, pauses and seeks it, and gets their own stream links of the file it plays (prefix `mls_g`, which a token's hex never holds) and conversions, held to the party's guest cap and the way's own limit; a link works only while the guest is in the party and it plays that file, checked at the same gate as every stream link. A guest never changes what plays and never sees an account's name. Their pass and links end with the party, when they leave, or when anyone in it sends them away. A way in may open only `party`: guests' routes and links, and nothing of the household's.

Why: A friend should be able to watch along without an account or any other access. A pass that reaches one party and its current file, ending with it, gives them only that; keeping the secret out of URLs a server sees keeps it out of logs, and a party-only way in lets a public address carry guests without opening the library.
