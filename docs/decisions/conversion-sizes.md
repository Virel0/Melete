Decision: A conversion held to a bitrate gets its picture height from one ladder: up to 4 Mbit/s 720p, up to 10 Mbit/s 1080p, up to 20 Mbit/s 1440p, and above that the source's size for HEVC but never more than 1440p for H.264. The height fits the rung's 16:9 box, a height asked for still holds (the smaller wins), and nothing is scaled up. Scaling comes before tone mapping. While any conversion runs for a viewer, background media work (seek-bar previews, intros and credits, trailers' sound, Make room's compressing) starts no new file. Amends decision 356: a watch party guest's player chooses as the household's does, playing the file as it is when the browser plays it and it's within the party's cap and the guest's own quality, copying its picture when only the container or the audio is the problem, and converting otherwise.

Why: A 4K HDR source held to an 8 Mbit/s way was encoded at 4K, tone mapping every 4K pixel, and ran barely faster than real time with two viewers on one GPU: the bits were spread too thin to look good, and the work was four times what a 1080p picture needs. Playback someone is watching can't wait; background work can. A guest's 1080p H.264 file well within the cap was converted all the same, costing the encoder for nothing.
